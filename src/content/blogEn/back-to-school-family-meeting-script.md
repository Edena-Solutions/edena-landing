<strong>The start-of-year meeting with families: script, invitation, and follow-up</strong>

<br>

The start-of-year meeting is the first impression the tutor gives families, and often the only time in the year when they are all in the same room. What is explained there, and how it is explained, shapes how that group will communicate for months.

This article proposes a 45-minute script, how to call the meeting so most people come, and what to do afterwards so the information also reaches those who could not attend.

<br>

<strong>When to hold it</strong>

<br>

The usual practice is to call it in the first three or four weeks of term. Earlier, the tutor does not yet know the group; later, the questions have already been answered by other routes, usually the families' WhatsApp group.

In the first year of a stage, such as the first year of early years, year 1 of primary, or year 1 of ESO, it is worth holding it as soon as possible: these are the families with the most questions, and the ones who most need to know how the school works.

<br>

<strong>Calling it so people come</strong>

<br>

Attendance depends above all on three things: notice, the time, and the reminder.

- **Notice:** at least two weeks, so families can organise themselves.
- **Time:** the end of the afternoon usually works better than mid-afternoon. If the school has many families with difficult schedules, offering an online link or a second date helps.
- **Confirmation of attendance:** asking for it lets you plan the room and know who will need the summary.
- **Reminder:** one 48 hours before and another on the day. A mobile alert works much better than a note in the backpack.
- **Language:** if there are families who do not speak the school's language well, a translated invitation and summary make the difference.

<br>

<strong>A 45-minute script</strong>

<br>

1. **Introducing the team (5 minutes):** tutor, specialists, and the school's points of contact. Who is who, and what to go to each of them for.
2. **What the year will look like (10 minutes):** aims of the stage, how the timetable is organised, planned outings and activities, and key dates.
3. **How we will communicate (10 minutes):** the official channel, what is communicated there, at what times, and within what time a reply is given. This is the most important block, and the one most often forgotten.
4. **Assessment and follow-up (5 minutes):** how assessment works, when reports arrive, and how to ask for a tutor meeting.
5. **Rules and services (5 minutes):** punctuality, absences, materials, meals and after-school activities, and where to look all of this up.
6. **Questions (10 minutes):** on general matters. Particular ones are referred to a tutor meeting.

<br>

<strong>The communication block, in detail</strong>

<br>

If the meeting were only for one thing, it should be to make clear how the school communicates. It is worth explaining:

- **What the official channel is** for notices, circulars, authorisations, and messages with the tutor.
- **What is not an official channel.** The families' WhatsApp group can be useful for organising themselves, but it is not the way to talk to the school. We cover this in <a href="/en/blog/parent-whatsapp-groups-official-school-channel/">parent WhatsApp groups</a>.
- **Hours:** when notices are sent and within what time messages are answered.
- **How to report an absence** and what to do in an emergency.
- **Photos and authorisations:** which images are shared and through which channel. We develop this in <a href="/en/blog/student-image-consent-school-year-2026-2027/">student image consent</a>.

Showing on screen what a notice looks like in the app, or how an absence is justified, saves a lot of questions in October.

<br>

<strong>What is worth avoiding</strong>

<br>

- **Reading a presentation** full of text. Better a few slides, and the full document sent afterwards.
- **Discussing individual cases in public**, especially if they affect a student's health, behaviour, or attainment.
- **Running long.** A meeting of an hour and a half loses half the room from minute fifty.
- **Leaving questions to the end with no time.** Families leave with doubts and resolve them on WhatsApp.

<br>

<strong>After the meeting</strong>

<br>

Follow-up is what turns a meeting into useful information for the whole group:

- **A summary the same day:** a one-page document with the essentials, plus the presentation, sent to every family through the official channel.
- **Read confirmation:** knowing which families have received and read the summary means you only call those who have not.
- **An attendance record:** useful for the tutor and for spotting families who are harder to reach.
- **Election of class representatives:** if it was done at the meeting, say who they are and how to contact them.

A few weeks later, a short survey on how the year has started gives valuable information; we explain it in <a href="/en/blog/family-satisfaction-nps-surveys-schools-2026/">satisfaction surveys for families</a>.

<br>

<strong>Differences by stage</strong>

<br>

- **Nursery:** more focused on routines, the settling-in period, food and rest, and how families will receive the day's information.
- **Primary:** organisation, homework, assessment, and the student's independence.
- **Secondary:** options, assessment by subject, guidance, and the use of devices.

<br>

<strong>Conclusion</strong>

<br>

A good start-of-year meeting is measured by how many families afterwards know how to communicate with the school. Notice in advance, a 45-minute script with the communication block well explained, and a summary the same day to every family are enough to start the year on a good footing.
